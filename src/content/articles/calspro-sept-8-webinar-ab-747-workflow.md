## What AB 747 (the SPARE Act) Actually Changes

Assembly Bill 747, also known as the Service of Process Accountability, Reform and Equity (SPARE) Act, takes effect **January 1, 2027**. The core requirements every California process server needs to know:

- **GPS-verified photo on every service attempt** — successful or not. This means embedded EXIF data with coordinates and a timestamp, not just a note in your log.
- **Stricter diligence standards** for substituted service. You'll need documented attempts at different times, including at least one attempt at a residence.
- **Public process server registries** — your registration information will be publicly searchable by anyone, including opposing counsel and the public.
- **Proof of service forms** will need to reflect the new documentation standard.

These aren't soft guidelines. They're statutory requirements with teeth. A proof of service that doesn't meet the standard can be challenged — and your registration can be affected.

## What You Should Be Doing Before September 8

Don't wait for the webinar to start adapting. Here's what to have in place now:

**1. Pick a GPS photo tool and start using it.**
Apps like Timestamp Camera and GeoMarker let you capture compliant photos today — embedded GPS coordinates, visible timestamp overlay, no extra hardware required. The best tool is the one you'll actually use on every serve. Start building the habit now, 16 months before the deadline.

**2. Audit your current proof-of-service forms.**
Look at your POS template and identify where GPS photo documentation would reference. If your form has no field or mechanism for it, that's your first fix.

**3. Document your diligence attempts more granularly.**
Even if you're not required to yet, start noting the exact time, location, and result of each attempt. When 2027 hits, you'll have a workflow already built — not a scramble.

**4. Verify your registration is current.**
The new public registry means your registration must be active and clean. Verify your county registration is up to date before the spotlight gets brighter.

**5. Register for the webinar.**
It's free. It's 45–60 minutes. You'll hear from peers who've already started thinking through implementation. That's worth your time.
